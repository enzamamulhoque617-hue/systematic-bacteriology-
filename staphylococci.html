Staphylococci

Exam-oriented interactive notes based on the provided Systematic Bacteriology class notes.

01. Introduction

Staphylococci are important pathogenic bacteria. The class notes describe Staphylococcus as an opportunistic pathogen and mention that more than 60 species are known.

Remember: Staphylococcus → important opportunistic pathogen.

02. Morphology

Gram reaction Gram-positive
Shape Spherical cocci
Arrangement Clusters resembling a bunch of grapes
Spore Does not produce spores

03. Cell Structure

  • Cell wall: thick peptidoglycan layer.
  • Teichoic acid: associated with the bacterial cell wall.
  • Lipoteichoic acid: associated with the cell envelope.
  • Capsule: many pathogenic bacteria may possess a polysaccharide capsule.
  • Pili: involved in attachment to host cells.
  • Flagella: the notes state that Staphylococcus does not produce spores; Staphylococci are described as non-motile.

04. Cultural Characteristics

  • Temperature: about 35–37°C.
  • pH: approximately 7.2–7.6.
  • Atmosphere: facultative anaerobic growth.
  • After incubation: colonies become large, smooth, opaque and creamy.
  • Nutrient agar: may show a characteristic appearance.
  • Blood agar: colonies may show haemolysis.
  • Selective medium: Mannitol Salt Agar is mentioned in the notes for isolation of S. aureus.

05. Biochemical Reaction

Coagulase test is highlighted in the class notes. It is used to detect coagulase production.

Coagulase test:
The notes describe two methods:
  • Slide coagulase test
  • Tube coagulase test

06. Pathogenesis

The notes describe two major ways in which Staphylococcus produces disease:

Infection The organism enters through damaged skin, mucosa or tissue.
Intoxication Disease may result from toxins produced by the organism.

Important virulence factors mentioned in the notes include:

  • Cell-associated factors
  • Cell-associated proteins
  • Clumping factor
  • Toxins
  • Enzymes contributing to virulence

07. Toxins

  • Haemolysins: toxins associated with damage to red blood cells.
  • Enterotoxin: associated with food poisoning and gastrointestinal symptoms.
  • Toxic shock syndrome toxin: associated with toxic shock syndrome.
  • Exfoliative toxin: associated with separation of skin layers and scalded skin syndrome.

09. Laboratory Diagnosis

1. Specimen

The specimen collected depends on the type and site of infection. The notes mention wound material, pus and nasal swabs among relevant specimens.

2. Microscopy

Direct microscopy with Gram staining can demonstrate Gram-positive cocci arranged in clusters.

3. Culture

Diagnosis can be supported by culture on appropriate media. Colony characteristics are then observed.

Exam flow:

Specimen → Gram stain → Gram-positive cocci in clusters → Culture → Biochemical testing → Identification
